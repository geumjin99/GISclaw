GISclaw is released under the **GNU Affero General Public License v3.0 or
later** (see [`LICENSE`](LICENSE)). For most people that is all you need, and it
costs nothing.

A separate **commercial licence** is available from the copyright holder for
those who cannot accept the AGPL's reciprocity requirement.

## You do *not* need a commercial licence to

- Run GISclaw, unmodified or modified, for any purpose — including inside a
  company, including on paid work, including on client projects.
- Use it in academic research, teaching, or a thesis.
- Modify it for your own internal use, with no obligation to publish anything.
- Redistribute it, or run a modified version as a public service, **provided you
  release your version's complete corresponding source under the AGPL too**.

Commercial use is *not* restricted by the AGPL. Only closed-source
redistribution is.

## You probably *do* need one if you want to

- Embed GISclaw, in whole or in part, in a **proprietary product** you ship or
  sell without releasing its source.
- Operate a **modified GISclaw as a hosted or SaaS offering** while keeping your
  modifications closed (AGPL §13 would otherwise require you to offer the
  corresponding source to every user of that service).
- Redistribute GISclaw under terms other than the AGPL — for example, to a
  customer whose procurement policy prohibits copyleft dependencies.
- Obtain a warranty, indemnification, or support commitment. The AGPL grant
  comes with none of these.

If you are unsure whether your use requires one, ask — the answer is often "no".

## What a commercial licence typically grants

Terms are agreed per engagement, but a commercial licence generally provides a
non-exclusive right to use and distribute GISclaw **without** the AGPL's
source-disclosure obligations, optionally together with support, priority fixes,
or private domain skill packs.

Pricing depends on the scale and nature of the deployment. Academic,
non-profit, and public-sector users should ask about reduced or waived fees.

## Why this arrangement exists

GISclaw is developed as open research and stays open — the AGPL guarantees that
anyone can read it, run it, learn from it, and build on it, and that
improvements shipped to the public come back to the commons.

The commercial licence exists so that the project can also be used in settings
where copyleft is genuinely not workable, without the code becoming closed for
everyone else. The copyright holder retains full ownership of the work and can
therefore offer both.

## A note on contributions

Dual licensing only works while one party can license the whole work, so pull
requests come with a licence grant — you keep your copyright, and your
contribution is published to everyone under the AGPL like the rest of the
project. The details are in [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md).
